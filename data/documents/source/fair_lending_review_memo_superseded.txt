SUBJECT: Fair-Lending Review Procedures for Credit Decisions

PURPOSE

This memorandum establishes internal review procedures for identifying potential fairness and compliance concerns in the loan-review process.

The memorandum does not replace applicable law, regulation, or official regulatory guidance.

REVIEW PRINCIPLES

Loan applications should be evaluated using the underwriting criteria applicable to the selected product and policy period.

The decision process should be consistent, documented, and traceable.

REVIEW TRIGGERS

A loan application should be considered for additional compliance review when one or more of the following conditions exists:

1. The decision appears inconsistent with the current underwriting policy.

2. Applicants with materially similar financial characteristics appear to have received materially different treatment without a documented business reason.

3. A manual override changes an automated decision without adequate documentation.

4. The reason for an adverse decision cannot be clearly identified.

5. The underwriting record contains information suggesting that an inappropriate applicant characteristic may have influenced the decision.

6. A loan officer or reviewer identifies inconsistent application of product eligibility requirements.

7. A current policy requirement appears to have been replaced by an older or superseded document during the decision process.

8. The system retrieves a document whose authority status is DRAFT, SUPERSEDED, or EXPIRED when a CURRENT document is available.

DOCUMENT REVIEW

When reviewing a potential compliance issue, the reviewer should identify:

* Applicant identifier
* Application date
* Loan product
* Credit/risk tier
* Requested amount
* Applicable underwriting policy
* Applicable product eligibility rules
* Decision outcome
* Manual-review status
* Reason for escalation

VERSION AND AUTHORITY CHECK

The reviewer should confirm the following document metadata:

Document ID
Title
Effective Date
Version
Authority Status
Owner
Jurisdiction/Region

A document marked CURRENT should generally take precedence over a related document marked SUPERSEDED when evaluating a current application.

Historical documents may be used to understand previous decisions but should not automatically be treated as current policy.

MANUAL OVERRIDE REVIEW

When a reviewer overrides an automated decision, the reviewer should record:

* Original automated decision
* New decision
* Reason for override
* Evidence supporting the override
* Applicable policy section
* Reviewer identifier
* Review date

An undocumented override should be escalated for quality and governance review.

FAIR-LENDING WARNING SIGNS

The following patterns may warrant additional analysis:

* Different decisions for applicants with similar documented financial characteristics.
* Different loan terms without an identifiable underwriting explanation.
* Repeated manual overrides associated with one geographic region.
* Repeated exceptions to product eligibility requirements.
* Missing or inconsistent adverse-decision documentation.
* Use of outdated underwriting rules.
* Retrieval of conflicting policy documents.

ESCALATION LEVELS

LOW:
Documentation issue that does not appear to affect the final decision.

MEDIUM:
Potential inconsistency in underwriting or product eligibility.

HIGH:
Potential compliance concern, unexplained decision difference, significant policy conflict, or repeated override pattern.

REQUIRED ACTION

When a potential fair-lending concern is identified, the reviewer should preserve the relevant evidence and escalate the matter according to the Loan Approval and Escalation Procedure.
