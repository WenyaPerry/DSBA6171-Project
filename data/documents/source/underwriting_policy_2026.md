# 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the underwriting standards used by Meridian Financial Services to evaluate consumer loan applications.

The purpose of the policy is to provide consistent criteria for evaluating applicant financial information, credit risk, requested loan amounts, and loan-product eligibility.

This policy is designed for use by both automated underwriting systems and employees conducting manual loan reviews.

---

# 2. Scope

This policy applies to consumer loan applications submitted through Meridian Financial Services.

The policy applies to:

* Personal loans
* Auto loans
* Debt-consolidation loans
* Home-improvement loans
* Other consumer loan products designated by the Lending Risk Management team

Applications outside the approved product scope must be referred for manual review.

---

# 3. Applicant Eligibility

## 3.1 Minimum Income

Applicants must demonstrate annual income of at least **$40,000** unless a product-specific requirement establishes a higher threshold.

Income may be evaluated using documented employment income and other permitted sources of income according to applicable procedures.

Applications below the minimum income requirement should not receive automatic approval.

## 3.2 Employment History

Applicants should generally demonstrate at least **12 months of continuous employment history**.

Employment history of less than 12 months requires additional documentation or manual review.

## 3.3 Credit Risk Tier

Applicants are assigned a credit risk tier based on the credit information available to the underwriting system.

The standard risk tiers are:

* Tier A — Lowest modeled credit risk
* Tier B — Standard credit risk
* Tier C — Elevated credit risk
* Tier D — High credit risk

Product-specific requirements determine which tiers qualify for automatic approval.

---

# 4. Loan Amount Requirements

## 4.1 Maximum Loan Amount

The maximum amount available to an applicant depends on the selected loan product.

The underwriting system must compare the applicant's requested amount against the maximum amount defined in the current loan-product eligibility table.

## 4.2 High-Dollar Applications

Applications requesting more than **$50,000** must receive manual review unless a documented product exception applies.

The reviewer must confirm:

1. Applicant eligibility.
2. Requested loan amount.
3. Income documentation.
4. Credit risk tier.
5. Debt-to-income information.
6. Applicable product requirements.

---

# 5. Debt-to-Income Review

The underwriting system should calculate the applicant's debt-to-income ratio when sufficient information is available.

Applications exceeding the product-specific debt-to-income threshold must be referred for manual review.

A high debt-to-income ratio does not by itself establish a final adverse decision. The reviewer must evaluate the application according to the applicable product and underwriting requirements.

---

# 6. Product Eligibility

Before an approval decision is issued, the underwriting system must verify that the applicant qualifies for the selected loan product.

The system should evaluate:

* Minimum credit tier
* Minimum income
* Maximum requested amount
* Debt-to-income threshold
* Geographic restrictions
* Required documentation
* Required level of review

The current product eligibility document is:

**FIN-RULE-001 — Loan Product Eligibility Rules, Version 2026.1**

If multiple versions of the product rules are available, the system must use the document marked **CURRENT** and must not use a document marked **SUPERSEDED** for a current decision.

---

# 7. Fair-Lending Requirements

## 7.1 Consistent Evaluation

Applicants must be evaluated using the approved underwriting criteria applicable to their loan product.

The underwriting system and employees must not intentionally use prohibited applicant characteristics as decision criteria.

## 7.2 Consistent Treatment

Applicants with materially similar financial and credit characteristics should be evaluated using the same applicable underwriting rules.

Differences in treatment must be supported by documented, business-relevant factors.

## 7.3 Compliance Review

Applications may be referred to Compliance when:

* The decision appears inconsistent with the current underwriting policy.
* A manual override lacks sufficient documentation.
* A reviewer identifies a potential prohibited decision factor.
* The reason for an adverse decision cannot be adequately documented.

---

# 8. Automated Decisions

Applications may receive an automated decision when all required information is available and the application satisfies the applicable product and underwriting criteria.

Possible automated outcomes are:

* APPROVED
* DECLINED
* MANUAL_REVIEW

An automated decision must be traceable to the applicable underwriting rules.

---

# 9. Manual Review

An application must be referred to manual review when:

* Required information is missing.
* The applicant falls outside an automatic approval threshold.
* The requested amount exceeds the automated approval limit.
* The credit information contains a material inconsistency.
* The selected product has a mandatory manual-review requirement.
* A compliance concern is identified.

The reviewer must document the reason for manual review and the evidence used to reach the final decision.
